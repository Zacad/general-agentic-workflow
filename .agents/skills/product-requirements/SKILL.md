# Product requirements

**Trigger:** An assigned software-product scope is nontrivial or materially changing, and unresolved user-visible behavior or consequential choices warrant deeper discovery. During a new root's authorized framing, clarify enough to draft its eventual-outcome brief; after a checkpoint, deepen discovery within the approved scope. For substantial nonsoftware or materially changing nonsoftware requirements, use `endeavor-requirements`; for a small bounded issue with clear existing criteria, use the relevant lifecycle skill and proportionate brief/leaf coverage instead. This skill does not require a separate PRD or child in every case and grants no authority to produce or approve one. Apply the protocol's shared current-input adequacy, precise presented review and coverage/readiness contract alongside the software-specific method below.

**Inputs:** User's exact request and available answers, relevant existing requirements and evidence, unit/revision and actual root/parent checkpoint where required, applicable decisions, owned artifact/report paths, stakeholders and known constraints. Use `critical-reasoning` and the relevant entry/lifecycle skill; use `choose-approach` for consequential alternatives, not as a substitute for product discovery.

**Procedure:**
   **Material UI quality:** early in discovery, ask a few useful questions about desired visual polish/taste/contextual success, actual audience/tasks/setting, platform/devices/input/AT, current brand/assets/system and liked/disliked references with reasons. Record actual source/status, not an assumed aesthetic. Load `ui-ux-design` early enough to explore contextual alternatives and genuine viewable previews before affected production; requirements elicit quality, UI expertise explores it, architecture compares technical feasibility without settling taste. Missing brand may be a labelled proposed contextual default for visual review, not agreed plain styling or omitted visuals. Material missing context needs question/owner/action. Consume sufficient scoped visual response or validated current reviewed reuse/tiny basis under the protocol's visual-review gate before affected dependent readiness; preserve open/partial/declined scope and continue unrelated authorized work.

1. Trace each material statement to its source and status: **requested** (user actually said it), **constraint** (credible external source with citation), **proposed** (worker suggestion, including defaults), **agreed** (specific user-reviewed choice with actual approval reference and applicability), **excluded** (user-agreed or approved scope exclusion, otherwise label proposed exclusion), or **open/unknown**. A checkpoint on a root approves only its stated scope and defaults, not every plausible behavior; reproduce those defaults as **agreed within that root** with its checkpoint reference, even if initially proposed by a worker. Do not infer later user preference from approval of a different scope. Where sources disagree, preserve both and ask.
2. Elicit a few representative scenarios in the product's own domain: actors and permissions, entry/preconditions, core task and observable result, alternatives and terminal outcomes, error/cancel/abandonment paths, recovery and retention. Ask who initiates/refuses/reverses each ending and how it affects completion, resumability, identity/ownership, history, ranking or other domain consequences *when relevant*. In a draughts/checkers product, explicitly ask whether resignation, voluntary pass, loss when no legal move exists, forfeiture (and trigger), agreed draw and automatic draws are wanted; who can invoke/refuse each, and what each does to saves, finished history and scoring. Some may conflict with the chosen ruleset; none is universally required. For other products, derive analogous **explicit user questions**, not just conditional acceptance examples: can a core action, its confirmation, or cancellation fail; what happens after disconnect/reload leaves success uncertain; can retries duplicate or compete with another action? Ask about reversal, recovery, user-visible state and retained records without presuming a remedy or importing a specific domain template.
3. Ask which scenarios are in scope or out, who sees and owns data, whether local or shared identities/results matter, recovery after failure or reload, and quality constraints such as accessibility, supported devices, latency, privacy, durability or operations where material. For each agreed outcome give a concrete user-visible acceptance example (starting state → action/exception → observable result, including saved/finished state as applicable); make unresolved examples conditional. Separate desired product behavior and quality drivers from implementation choices. Leave runtime, stack, deployment, storage mechanism and detailed test strategy open for later architecture/verification review; ask only product-level constraints needed to compare them later.
4. Make a compact reviewable PRD or equivalent in the assigned path: goal/boundary; source/status table; scenario/exception matrix with observable examples; quality constraints and exclusions; open questions with alternatives, consequence/dependency and decision owner. Ask the user to review consequential proposals and resolve blocking open choices explicitly. Update the artifact only with actual responses, recording exact reference and scope. Where a prior approved root already fixes a default, identify it as root-agreed; a new conflicting preference needs an appropriate revision/decision and checkpoint before affected work. A draft PRD, silence, declined answer or agent recommendation is not authorization. Do not force certainty about unresolved requirements; note which dependent work must wait and what independent scoped work may proceed.

**Output:** Short user-reviewable specification (or proportionate additions to an existing brief when enough), questions prioritized by consequence and dependency, source/status references, observable acceptance examples and handoff in the owned report. End with an explicit authority handoff: **affected unit ID@revision** (or “ID/revision to assign” if none exists); blocking choice and dependent work paused; **owner/action: orchestrator to request the user's review and record the applicable named root checkpoint, parent checkpoint for a child, or revised scope/decision checkpoint if material change requires one**. State when an existing checkpoint already covers unchanged scope; do not demand a new checkpoint for a routine answer within it. A PRD or broad root approval cannot authorize an unnamed child or materially changed scope. Existing brief/checkpoint/decision records remain authoritative; do not create or edit them without assigned ownership. Routine reversible implementation choices inside approved boundaries remain delegated.

**Stop/escalate:** Essential answer is unanswered or declined and affects the next outcome; contradictory evidence or a newly proposed requirement exceeds the approved boundary; a material inherited choice would change; or ownership/checkpoint is unclear. Stop affected advancement, identify the exact question and impacted scope for the orchestrator/user, and continue only independent authorized work. Never mark a proposed choice agreed or begin dependent build because a PRD draft exists.

**Preparation handoff:** after adequate requirements and appropriate software structure (architecture when material), expose the broad high-level horizon and explicitly choose a bounded delivery group with the user per protocol. Project-specific proposed taxonomy helps explain relationships, not prove sizing or impose universal levels. Lead names/context before exact IDs/revisions. Supply scenario/coverage readiness, open dependencies and deferral reasons, owners/actions, dependency impacts and reconsideration basis to the coordinating Work split/Relationships writer for sibling-level waves. Independently acceptable modules/infrastructure and vertical slices remain semantic candidates; do not prebuild all descendants. Requirements/discovery/structure/preparation outputs establishing readiness are exempt from the dependent-delivery barrier under their own authority, with truthful open-review limits. Selection/review does not authorize child entry or replace own-unit investigation.
